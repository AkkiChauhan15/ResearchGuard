Mechanistic Rationale for Dual Target Inhibition

KRAS(G12D) cycling between inactive GDP-bound and active GTP-bound states remains partially dynamic despite oncogenic stabilization. MRTX1133 locks the switch-II pocket in the GDP state with sub-nanomolar affinity, but mono-inhibition universally induces rapid adaptive resistance within 48 to 72 hours.

device_hub 1. RTK Reactivation Loop

Inhibition of downstream ERK1/2 abrogates native negative-feedback phosphorylation on upstream Receptor Tyrosine Kinases (predominantly EGFR, HER2, and FGFR1). This unleashes hyper-active SOS1/2 nucleotide exchange complexes.

Pathway: KRAS(G12D) → pERK⊥ → EGFR Hyperphosphorylation
hub 2. SHP2 (PTPN11) Scaffolding

SHP2 acts as an indispensable convergent allosteric bridge connecting phosphorylated RTK cytoplasmic domains to SOS1/GRB2. Combining MRTX1133 with allosteric SHP2 inhibitors (SHP099, RMC-4550) uncouples the signaling relay before wild-type RAS GTP exchange happens.

Target: Allosteric Tunnel Lock (PTP Domain Stabilizer)
science Known Caveats & Unresolved Experimental Vulnerabilities
  • Systemic cumulative hepatotoxicity and mucosal desquamation have complicated early-phase combinatory dosing regimes.
  • Monolayer in vitro IC50 measurements dramatically overestimate efficacy inside densely fibrotic, hypoxic desmoplastic pancreatic microenvironments.
  • Parallel activation of wild-type HRAS and NRAS isoforms under RTK hyperstimulation can sustain downstream PI3K/AKT axis independently of KRAS(G12D).
